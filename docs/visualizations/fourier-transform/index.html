Fourier Transform

Every complicated wave can hide simpler rhythms. Build a signal, discover its frequencies, and reconstruct it from the Fourier spectrum.

The frequency laboratory

The top graph is the sampled signal; the bottom graph is its computed Fourier spectrum. Try changing the second wave's phase: the waveform changes, but its spectral magnitude does not.

Frequency domain amplitude vs hertz

Model: one second of ideal sampled data, zero initial offset, single-sided amplitudes corrected for the selected window's coherent gain. A window changes the analyzed samples; inverse reconstruction displays the windowed sequence when Hann is selected. No microphone or measured audio is used.

References and further reading

  • Steven W. Smith, The Scientist and Engineer's Guide to Digital Signal Processing — freely accessible introduction.
  • MIT OpenCourseWare, Signals and Systems — university lectures and exercises.
  • Oppenheim and Schafer, Discrete-Time Signal Processing, 3rd ed., Pearson, 2010 — DFT, windows and sampling.
  • Bracewell, The Fourier Transform and Its Applications, 3rd ed., McGraw-Hill, 2000 — continuous transforms.
  • Mallat, A Wavelet Tour of Signal Processing, 3rd ed., Academic Press, 2009 — time-frequency analysis.
