PHYSICS · CLASSICAL OPTICS · WAVE INTERFERENCE

Classical double-slit experiment

Why does light passing through two narrow openings make a pattern of bright and dark bands? Change the experiment and watch waves reinforce or cancel one another.

Young's interference laboratory

Predict: when the slits move farther apart, will the bright stripes spread out or move closer together?

All images are calculations, not photographs. The ray diagram is schematic and not to scale. Pattern brightness is normalized separately for each configuration.

Approximate fringe spacing
3.67 mm

Four learning levels

Experiments

  1. Double slit separation. Observe how the fringe spacing changes.
  2. Change red light to violet light. Which produces closer fringes?
  3. Open only one slit. The fine two-slit fringes disappear, but the broad diffraction envelope remains.
  4. Widen each slit. Observe the narrowing envelope; some interference maxima may disappear under diffraction minima.
  5. Increase screen distance. Test whether the central fringe spacing scales approximately with distance.

Assumptions and limitations

This is a monochromatic, spatially coherent, scalar, far-field (Fraunhofer) model for two identical rectangular slits with uniform illumination. It uses the small-angle mapping of screen position to angle and ignores polarization, finite source coherence, detector response, near-field propagation and material effects. The controls keep slit width smaller than center separation. Intensity is normalized to its own central maximum, so comparing absolute throughput across one- and two-slit modes is not meaningful. It does not simulate individual photons or quantum measurement.
