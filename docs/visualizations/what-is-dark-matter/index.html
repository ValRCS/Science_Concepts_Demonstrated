EARTH & SPACE / COSMOLOGY

What is dark matter?

Astronomers can see stars orbiting galaxies. Their motion reveals gravity from much more matter than we can observe with telescopes. What could be making the difference?

✦ We have strong gravitational evidence for dark matter. We still do not know its microscopic identity.

02 / LEARN AT YOUR LEVEL

From discovery to research

Switch levels freely. The galaxy laboratory remains the same; deeper controls appear as you progress.

LEVEL 1 / DISCOVER & PLAY

How can you notice something you cannot see?

Imagine watching leaves move when you cannot feel the wind. The leaves show you that something is happening. Astronomers use the motion of stars in a similar way: their paths tell us about gravity.

Stars far from the centres of many galaxies travel surprisingly fast. The stars and gas we can see do not seem to provide enough gravity to explain those movements. Scientists call the missing source of gravity dark matter.

Try it! Move the tracked star toward the galaxy's edge. Now switch between “With dark halo” and “Visible mass only.” Which model predicts the faster orbit far out?

Important: Dark matter is not a black cloud or a shadow. It does not glow like stars, and we have not yet identified what it is made of.

LEVEL 2 / EXPLORE & UNDERSTAND

Why do galaxy rotation curves matter?

Gravity holds orbiting objects on curved paths. In a simplified spherical system, if nearly all the mass is concentrated closer to the centre, the circular orbital speed decreases farther out: v ∝ 1/√r. Astronomers instead find that the outer rotation curves of many spiral galaxies remain approximately flat over large ranges.

A dark-matter halo is a proposed extended distribution of unseen mass. Its additional gravity increases the circular speed, particularly in outer regions. Real galaxies also contain ordinary matter we cannot see directly in visible-light photographs, such as gas.

Investigate: Increase visible-matter mass, then increase halo speed. How do the two changes affect the orange and teal curves differently? Can you make the halo curve nearly flat in the outer galaxy?

Units: 1 kiloparsec (kpc) is about 3,260 light-years. Orbital speed is shown in kilometres per second (km/s). The mass slider uses units of ten billion solar masses, M☉.

Other evidence comes from the way mass bends light (gravitational lensing), colliding galaxy clusters, and the early-universe cosmic microwave background. These are independent observations, not outputs of our toy galaxy.

LEVEL 3 / ANALYZE & MODEL

A circular-speed model with two mass components

For a spherically symmetric gravitational mass distribution and a circular test-particle orbit, balancing centripetal acceleration against gravity gives:

vc²(r) / r = G M(<r) / r²,   hence   vc²(r) = G M(<r) / r.

We approximate luminous matter by a Hernquist spherical profile with total mass Mb and scale length a:

Mb(<r) = Mb r² / (r + a)²;   vb² = G Mb r / (r + a)².

The halo has a cored pseudo-isothermal density profile, with x = r/rc:

ρh(r) = ρ₀ / (1 + x²);   Mh(<r) = 4πρ₀rc³ (x − arctan x).
vh²(r) = v∞² (1 − arctan(x)/x);   v∞² = 4πGρ₀rc².

Gravitational accelerations add, so vtotal² = vb² + vh². In the simulation, G = 4.30091 × 10⁻⁶ kpc (km/s)² M☉⁻¹. The “visible only” switch sets the halo term to zero, while keeping the baryonic model unchanged.

Analyze: Raise the halo core radius while holding its asymptotic speed fixed. Where does the curve transition toward a plateau? Repeat with the baryonic scale length.

Assumptions: Newtonian gravity, spherical symmetry, circular test-particle orbits, equilibrium, and an invented smooth mass profile. Real spiral-galaxy stars lie primarily in a flattened disk, whose force law differs from this spherical proxy. The generated curves must not be interpreted as fits to real observations.

LEVEL 4 / INVESTIGATE & RESEARCH

What do the observations establish, and what remains uncertain?

Within the spherical circular-orbit approximation, a flat rotation curve vc(r) ≈ constant implies M(<r) ∝ r and therefore ρ(r) ∝ r⁻² over the relevant range. Our cored halo approaches that behavior at large r. Its mass grows without bound as r → ∞, so it is not a globally realistic finite halo; a truncated profile or another halo prescription is necessary for large-scale prediction.

Cosmological simulations often use Navarro–Frenk–White (NFW)-like cusped profiles rather than this classroom core. Observations and modeling at small radii are affected by baryonic feedback, non-circular motions, inclination uncertainty, stellar mass-to-light ratios, and parameter degeneracies. A rotation curve alone does not uniquely identify a particle or an inner density profile.

Evidence for a nonluminous gravitating component arises from multiple scales: galaxy kinematics, lensing mass maps, galaxy-cluster mergers, large-scale structure, and cosmic microwave background anisotropies. The Bullet Cluster shows a lensing mass distribution spatially offset from much of its hot X-ray gas, a powerful challenge to models relying only on ordinary matter. The ΛCDM model describes the cosmic microwave background remarkably well but assumes a particular cosmological framework.

Particle candidates include axions and weakly interacting massive particles. Nonparticle alternatives, including modified-gravity theories, are investigated and face the obligation to explain all the evidence consistently. As of November 2026 no dark-matter particle has a confirmed laboratory detection; an unusual LZ event reported in September 2026 was explicitly not a discovery claim.

Research exercise: Try to match an approximately flat outer curve using different visible masses and halo core radii. Which model parameters can the displayed curve constrain, and what extra observations (gas maps, lensing, stellar populations) would help break degeneracies?

Model boundaries: no observational uncertainties or instrument selection effects; no self-consistent disk dynamics, relativistic lensing, cosmological expansion, halo truncation, or formation history. Animation time has no cosmological scaling.

03 / OBSERVATIONS, NOT SIMULATIONS

How do scientists know?

Several independent kinds of measurement point to additional gravitating matter.

01

Galaxy rotation

Stars and gas in many galaxies orbit faster in their outskirts than expected from concentrated ordinary matter alone.

NASA: rotation evidence ↗
02

Gravitational lensing

The distortion of background light maps total gravitational mass, even where no corresponding luminous material is apparent.

NASA: lensing maps ↗
03

Colliding clusters

In the Bullet Cluster, the lensing-derived mass peaks are separated from much of the collision-heated ordinary gas.

NASA: Bullet Cluster ↗
04

The early universe

Patterns in the cosmic microwave background constrain the amount of ordinary and cold dark matter under ΛCDM.
