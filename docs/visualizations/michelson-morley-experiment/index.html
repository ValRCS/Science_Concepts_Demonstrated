Experiments to try

  1. Choose stationary ether. Rotate from 0° to 90°. What happens to the fringes? Rotate to 180°.
  2. At Level 2 or above, halve the assumed speed. The predicted shift falls to one quarter.
  3. Double the arm length. The predicted shift doubles. Try a shorter wavelength.
  4. Choose relativity: in this idealized vacuum model, rotating the instrument gives no shift.
  5. Load the 1887 preset and compare the roughly 0.4-fringe historical expectation with the much smaller reported result.

These values summarize Michelson and Morley's 1887 paper, not a new statistical reanalysis. The apparatus was not perfectly noiseless, and a near-null result is not the same as a mathematically exact zero. This page does not fabricate observational points.

Historical milestones

1881: Michelson's first test. 1887: Michelson and Morley's more sensitive measurement. 1889–1904: FitzGerald and Lorentz developed contraction-based ether explanations. 1905: Einstein formulated special relativity without requiring a stationary luminiferous ether. Today: precision experiments test Lorentz symmetry.

The experiment did not, by itself, logically prove special relativity or eliminate every conceivable ether theory. Its importance lies in the constraints it placed on the classical stationary-ether picture.
