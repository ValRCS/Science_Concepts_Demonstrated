Experiments to try

  1. Start annihilation with zero kinetic energy. Why are the two photons emitted in opposite directions?
  2. Increase kinetic energy: how does the energy of each photon change in the center-of-momentum frame?
  3. Switch to pair production. Compare two photons at the threshold and above it. Why can one isolated photon not make a pair in vacuum?
  4. Build antihydrogen and compare its constituents with ordinary hydrogen.
  5. In PET mode, watch the two photon detections. Why is the line between them only an approximate indication of the annihilation location?
  6. Try zero antimatter deficit and then a small deficit in the counting model. How much matter survives?

Model boundaries and conservation laws

ExperimentWhat the model does—and does not—represent
AnnihilationIdeal two-photon electron–positron channel, head-on equal and opposite momenta in the center-of-momentum frame. Other channels and radiative corrections are omitted.
Pair productionTwo equal-energy head-on photons in vacuum. Threshold: 1.022 MeV total. A single isolated photon cannot create a pair in vacuum.
AntihydrogenOne antiproton and one positron form a neutral anti-atom; orbital depiction is symbolic, not a classical trajectory.
PETIdealized 180° photon emission and coincidence detections; positron range, non-collinearity, attenuation and detector response are omitted.
Cosmic imbalanceSimple pair counting, not a simulation of expanding-universe kinetics or a mechanism of baryogenesis.
